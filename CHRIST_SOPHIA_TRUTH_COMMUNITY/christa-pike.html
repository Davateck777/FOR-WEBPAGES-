Christa Pike survived her execution. What happened?

On the night of 30 September 2026, Tennessee strapped Christa Pike to a gurney to become the first woman the state had executed in 200 years. The team spent about an hour trying to find a vein, using at least seven needles in her left arm. Then they pushed two doses of pentobarbital — each one supposed to be lethal.

She was still alive. Snoring, loudly, while the reporters chosen to witness it looked at each other wondering what was going on. 😶

Who she is, in one line 🧍‍♀️

Christa Pike was sentenced to death for the 1995 murder of 19-year-old Colleen Slemmer. Nothing on this page is an argument that she is innocent, or that what she did wasn't terrible. The question here is narrower and uglier: what happens when the state tries to kill someone and fails?

🕰️ The night, minute by minute

As recorded by Tori Gessner of WKRN, one of the media witnesses. She was originally scheduled to die that morning; last-minute appeals pushed it to the evening.

    Witnesses are brought into the witness chamber.

  2. 7:26 p.m.

    The blinds onto the execution chamber open. Pike gives her final statement.

  3. 7:34 p.m.

    Pike says her arm feels like it's about to burst. Over the next minutes she appears to be talking, breathing, moaning — and snoring.

  4. 7:46 p.m.

    The blinds are closed. 🚪

  5. 7:49 p.m.

    Blinds reopen. Pike is snoring with her mouth open. Still alive.

  6. 8:05 p.m.

    Blinds go down again — and stay down.

  7. 8:53 p.m.

    Witnesses are escorted out. Pike has not been declared dead. Nobody in the room knows what has happened.

  8. Thursday

    Her legal team says she is in critical condition, receiving life-saving medical care at an off-site facility. 🏥

🔍 Why did it fail?

Tap a card. Her lawyers named four problems; experts added the bigger one.

🩸 Difficult vein access & blown veins

An hour of attempts, at least seven needles in one arm. If the drug doesn't go cleanly into a vein it pools in tissue instead — which is also what "my arm feels like it's about to burst" tends to mean.

🧪 Degraded pentobarbital

The protocol requires 100 ml of a 50 mg/ml solution — 5 grams — checked for contents and expiry 30 days before. Her lawyers argue the drug itself had degraded. Two full doses failed to kill her.

🚑 No emergency medical care on site

When it went wrong there was nothing in place to respond. She had to be transported to an off-site medical facility — to be saved from an execution.

🕶️ A protocol "veiled in secrecy"

The 44-page document says "there will be no deviation from the Protocol except by order of the Commissioner." It describes a primary dose and a backup set "if the inmate is not deceased." There is no protocol for what happens if the second set doesn't work either.

🧟 The "Frankenstein version" of medicine

Prof. Corinna Barrett Lain (University of Richmond), author of Secrets of the Killing State, says lethal injection has "all of the delicacy and need for accuracy of the medical model, but then it's been warped to suit the executioner's needs." Questionable drugs, non-medical staff, poor veins. Its botch rate is twice that of other methods. "It's a recipe for disaster from the start."

📜 Has this ever happened before?

Barely. Prof. Austin Sarat (Amherst) notes that botched executions aren't rare, but an execution simply failing is. The closest parallel is Willie Francis, who survived the electric chair in Louisiana 80 years ago — and was executed on the second attempt. Sarat calls Pike "the first person to survive a lethal injection where the drugs reached her."

⏭️ So what happens now?

Nobody fully knows. Here's what's actually moving. Hover or tap a node.

⏸️ Executions paused

Gov. Bill Lee called off Tennessee's last remaining execution this year, pending a third-party review to "determine what exactly occurred." It's his second such pause — he did the same in 2022 over untested drugs.

🔎 Investigation opened

The Attorney General's office: "The Governor has appropriately called for an investigation. We need to know what happened."

🙏 Clemency request

Her lawyers want the governor to commute her sentence, arguing she has endured punishment "greater than any other person in the history of the American death penalty."

🇺🇸 The wider picture

27 states still have the death penalty. Lethal injection troubles have already pushed some toward nitrogen gas and the return of the firing squad. Tennessee also had a failed attempt in May 2026.

🧭 The uncomfortable bit

You can believe Christa Pike deserves to die and still find this alarming. A state that can't reliably do the thing it claims the legal right to do — that's not a philosophical problem, it's an engineering and honesty problem. Two doses, seven needles, an hour of searching, a curtain pulled shut, and a written protocol that simply stops at the point where it failed.

Whatever you think should happen to her next, "we need to know what happened" is the one line everyone in this story agrees on. 🙏
