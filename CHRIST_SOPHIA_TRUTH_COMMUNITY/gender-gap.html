🖼️ Visual slides — swipe through the argument

Five slides. Thirty seconds. Use the arrows, swipe, or arrow keys.

01

Testosterone isn't an "anger switch" 🔬

Harvard researchers put it plainly: testosterone drives a hunger for status and dominance, not rage by itself. The danger shows up when a society has millions of men chasing status and nothing to offer them.

02

Threatened men vote for war 🪖

Sociological work on "masculine overcompensation" found that when men with higher testosterone feel their status is challenged, their support for military action can roughly double. Diplomacy gets harder exactly when it's needed most.

03

Risk goes blind 🎲

High testosterone lowers sensitivity to punishment and fear. Add the "winner effect" — past victories making leaders underestimate cost — and you get reckless escalation instead of an off-ramp.

04

The youth bulge 💣

A large share of young, single men with no job, no property and no marriage prospects is a known instability pattern. Their status drive gets redirected: unrest, radicalisation, recruitment by armed groups.

05

Then women pay the bill 💔

Hudson & den Boer called these men "bare branches." Groups like ISIS and Boko Haram recruited them with an explicit offer: a wife, taken by force. UN reporting treats conflict-related sexual violence as a deliberate tactic, not random chaos.

⚠️ Five dangers, spelled out

Tap a card to open it.

1. More appetite for military action 🪖

When status feels threatened, support for war-first solutions jumps. Research on political psychology links higher testosterone to stronger "overcompensation" after a perceived challenge — meaning compromise reads as weakness during a standoff.

2. Broken risk assessment 🎲

Lower fear response + lower punishment sensitivity = dangerously high risk tolerance. Leaders riding a streak of wins stop pricing the real cost of escalation.

3. Empathy drains out of the room 🧊

Biomedical studies report reduced empathic mimicry and weaker reading of emotional cues. Diplomacy runs on nuance; without it, a neighbour's defensive move gets misread as an attack.

4. The youth bulge 👥

Single young men carry the highest baseline testosterone — mating and status competition. Deny a whole generation of them work, housing and marriage, and that drive gets redirected into militias, gangs and civil unrest.

5. Status-loss panic 📉

The dual-hormone hypothesis: high testosterone plus high stress flips behaviour from seeking status to defending it, viciously. A declining power can start a defensive war purely to avoid looking weak.

6. What that means for women in wartime 💔

Put it together: frustrated low-status men + collapse of law and order + armed groups that openly offer "a wife" as a recruitment bonus. Abductions, forced marriage and trafficking spike. UN agencies document sexual violence as a deliberate tool of control and terror, and conflict zones see a sharp rise in trafficking because war turns people into commodities.

Plainly: the men's status crisis gets paid for by women's bodies.

"The biological drive for status plus a domestic lack of opportunity creates a reservoir of frustrated men. Armed groups exploit exactly that frustration — promising status, money and women."

🧭 So what do we do with this?

None of this says men are the problem. It says men without a future are a predictable risk — and the people who pay first are women in conflict zones. The fix isn't mysterious either: jobs, education, housing, and marriage/family prospects for young men; hard prosecution of wartime sexual violence; and honest conversations like this one instead of pretending the numbers aren't there. 🙏
